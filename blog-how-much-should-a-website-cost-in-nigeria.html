How Much Should a Website Really Cost in Nigeria?

MT Mars Tee StudioWritten by the studio team
Share:

"How much does a website cost?" is one of the most common questions we get - and one of the hardest to answer honestly in one sentence, because the true answer is: it depends on what you actually need.

We've seen business owners quoted anywhere from ₦20,000 to well over ₦1,000,000 for what sounds like "a website." The gap isn't always about who's overcharging or undercharging - it's usually about what's actually being built.

What actually drives the price

A few things matter far more than most people expect:

  • Number of pages. A single-page site is a very different job from an 8-page site with a catalogue, portfolio, and contact system.
  • Custom design vs. templates. A fully custom design built around your brand takes real design time. A templated site is faster and cheaper, but looks like a template.
  • Backend features. Anything that needs a database - product listings you can update yourself, a review system, an admin dashboard - adds real engineering time.
  • Content readiness. If you already have your text, photos, and logo ready, your project moves faster. If we're creating content from scratch, that's additional work.

A realistic starting point

As a rough guide for the Nigerian market in 2026, a simple 1-3 page business website with no backend typically starts around ₦60,000. A more complete 4-8 page site with a proper design system, gallery, and contact handling sits comfortably in the ₦130,000 - ₦300,000 range. Anything involving a real database, admin dashboard, or e-commerce moves into ₦750,000 and up.

These aren't arbitrary numbers - they roughly track how our own catalogue tiers are structured, because the underlying cost drivers are the same no matter who builds it.

The real question to ask yourself

Instead of asking "what's the cheapest website I can get," ask "what does my business actually need to look credible and function well?" A ₦60,000 site that does its one job cleanly is a better investment than a ₦300,000 site with features you'll never use.
